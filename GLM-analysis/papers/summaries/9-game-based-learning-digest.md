# 9-game-based-learning — digest

**Effectiveness of AI-Supported Game-Based Learning: A Systematic Review (Behavioral Sciences 16(7):1050, 2026)** | 55 studies 2021–2026, only 7% high-quality; stealth assessment AUC 0.848–0.913, interpretable rule-based adaptation η²p=0.349, but LLM satisfaction ≠ learning gains and AI presence alone often moved engagement, not knowledge | L1/L6 + DR3/DR23 — design rule for the AI-as-junior-staff loop: low-frequency, goal-directed AI calls; assess from process logs, not surveillance.

**Game-Based Learning for Asynchronous AI Literacy Course (European J. of Education, 2026) ★** | 4,898 first-years, n=311 pre/post: significant cognitive (5.05→5.44), collaboration (4.94→5.37), self-efficacy (5.07→5.50, p<0.001) — but **ethics null (5.48→5.67, p=0.09)**; level-up points + leaderboard + peer-rated argumentation, 13 weeks | S0 AI-Fluency core + S5-L1 blueprint: copy level-up/peer-rating mechanics and ABCE outcome measurement; keep ethics in facilitated studio cases (L4); affective gains convert AI-skeptical students (DR27).

**Gamified AI-supported digital learning environments — meta-analysis (Frontiers in Education 11:1754080, 2026)** | 81 studies; pooled SMD=1.01 (I²=95.8%); achievement 1.86 / motivational 1.13 vs **behavioural engagement −0.30 (ns)**; generative AI 1.38; high-quality studies 0.84; Asia k=30 SMD=1.18 | L2 — gamified AI works as a teacher-orchestrated supplement, not a standalone wrapper; S0 tool-sprint blocks = "AI practice gym + faculty orchestration"; cite ~0.8 SD as the honest ceiling (DR24, L6).

**Integrating AI into educational game design: an AI-enhanced MDA framework (Research in Learning Technology 34:3746, 2026)** | conceptual, unvalidated — maps generative AI / adaptive algorithms / PCG onto all three MDA layers; criteria: generativity, adaptability, cognitive-load reduction, interactivity; numbers not reported | DR9 + DR15 — studio critique rubric: students must state which MDA layer each AI function touches (and which it must not); a Direction-Ladder taste→critique exercise, not evidence.

## Top 3 cases

1. **XJTLU gamified async AI-literacy course (★, S0 + S5-L1).** Level-up points (20/level to L6) + leaderboard + peer-rated collaborative argumentation over 13 weeks for 4,898 students → significant cognitive/behavioural/affective gains (self-efficacy 5.07→5.50, p<0.001) but ethics null (p=0.09). Adapt at DDCT as the fluency-course chassis; route ethics to case-based studio deliberation (L4); use affective gains as the lever for AI-hostile students (DR27).
2. **AI practice gym with teacher orchestration (L2, S0 tool-sprint blocks).** Meta-analytic: gamified AI supplements to teacher-led instruction pooled SMD=1.01 (genAI 1.38), but standalone engagement effects null (−0.30) and rigorous designs shrink to 0.84. Design sprint blocks as faculty-orchestrated practice gyms with explicit curricular alignment; set expectations at ~0.8 SD.
3. **Goal-directed, low-frequency AI-interaction rules (DR3 + DR23).** Across 55 studies: low-frequency goal-directed AI use beats habitual exploratory use; transparent rule-based adaptation η²p=0.349; stealth assessment from logs AUC 0.848–0.913; AI presence alone gave no knowledge advantage in several studies. Bake "call AI on purpose, log the call" into the brief→draft→critique→quality-gate loop + judgment notebook; grade process-visible work (L5), skip proctoring.

*Notes: all four papers are GBL-for-other-subjects (not game curriculum); near-zero longitudinal evidence beyond 8 weeks; Asia subgroup (k=30, SMD=1.18) is the closest regional estimate to Thailand; meta-analysis has internal table inconsistencies (ITS listed twice, DV mislabeled) — read subgroup numbers as approximate.*
