2024 · Computer Science Education in ChatGPT Era: Experiences from an Experiment in a Programming Course for Novice Programmers

Mathematics 12(5):629 (MDPI) · controlled between-subjects experiment, one full semester · n=182 first-year C++ OOP students (Univ. of Maribor; 2 groups ×99, 8 control users removed for protocol breach)
  • ChatGPT access changed nothing: lab work 65.27% vs 66.72% (p=0.760), midterms 65.96% vs 66.58% (p=0.856), final grade 65.93% vs 66.61% (p=0.730) — all null hypotheses accepted.
  • The null is credited to course redesign: ChatGPT-resistant assignments (UML diagrams, given code/main/I-O instead of rich text prompts), in-lab extension problems solved without AI, interactive oral defense (concept questions, code analysis, code changes, code completion), and paper-based midterms.
  • Redesign shifted usage, not just scores: only 21/69 treatment students used ChatGPT for every assignment; reported use favored code optimization/comparison over code generation. 70% of all students used ChatGPT regularly outside programming by June 2023.
  • ChatGPT itself scored 24/44 on the assignments without prompt engineering (~34 with expert prompting) — the defense requirement exists because raw output is grade-competitive.
DDCT: L5 (process visibility beats surveillance) + DR23 (assessment integrity) + S0 3-zone policy — the copyable package is "AI-permitted homework + AI-free in-lab extension + oral defense + paper exams"; it operationalizes a 🔴 zone that makes a permissive 🟢 zone safe. Authors warn the package needs periodic refresh as models improve (DR14 tool churn).

2025 · ChatGPT in Introductory Programming: Counterbalanced Evaluation of Code Quality, Conceptual Learning, and Student Perceptions

ACM SIGCITE ’25 · counterbalanced quasi-experiment (each group alternated AI / no-AI across two assignments) · n=27 CS1 students (C; groups of 14/13), multidimensional rubric, inter-rater κ=0.87
  • ChatGPT groups scored significantly higher on rubric-based code quality both times (functions: t=4.46, p=0.018; structures: t=5.02, p<0.001), especially correctness, readability, warning analysis — e.g. error handling 2.0 vs 0.05 on Assignment 2.
  • Completion time collapsed: 495s vs 2385s (A1, ~4.8×; t=−5.222, p=0.0002) and 868s vs 1934s (A2, ~2.2×; t=4.175, p=0.0011).
  • Conceptual learning was mixed: no overall difference on functions (U=153.5, p=0.323) except one "uses of functions" item (91.7% vs 0% correct, OR=∞); structures favored the ChatGPT group overall (U=77.0, p=0.0457) but no single question was significant.
  • Students valued debugging/practice help but worried about accuracy and long-term skill development; perceived learning skewed more positive in the first AI assignment than the second.
DDCT: L2 (integration mode is the variable) + DR23 + S0 tool-sprint labs — copy the design: counterbalanced AI-on/AI-off lab blocks with a published 5-category rubric (correctness / efficiency / readability / warning analysis / error handling) so quality gains are measurable, plus separate AI-free concept checks for the 🔴 zone. Small n=27; treat magnitudes as indicative.

2025 · Does Generative Artificial Intelligence Improve Students’ Higher-Order Thinking? A Meta-Analysis Based on 29 Experiments and Quasi-Experiments

Journal of Intelligence 13(12):160 (MDPI) · meta-analysis, 29 studies / 59 effect sizes, Hedges’s g, random effects (I²=77.3%)
  • Overall effect on higher-order thinking g=0.609 (95% CI 0.485–0.732); by dimension: problem-solving g=0.745 > critical thinking g=0.691 > creativity g=0.444 (all p<0.001).
  • Duration shows an inverted U: 8–16 weeks g=0.759 beats 0–8 weeks g=0.494 and >16 weeks g=0.372 (Q=9.106, p=0.011) — very long exposure breeds over-reliance.
  • Strongest moderator is the learner: high self-regulation g=0.863 vs low g=0.284 (Q=40.962, p<0.001). Pedagogy: project-based g=0.717 vs lecture-based g=0.396 (between-group n.s., p=0.232); K-12 g=0.857 vs post-secondary g=0.539 (n.s., p=0.067).
  • Egger’s test t=1.871, p=0.066 — mild publication bias cannot be ruled out.
DDCT: L6 (higher-order gains only medium → protect fundamentals) + L8 (block formats) — the 8–16-week sweet spot validates half-semester AI blocks (S0 3-week sprints may be too short to peak; S1/S4 studio-length blocks are in range), and the SRL gap says pair AI with self-regulation scaffolds (judgment notebook, reflection logs) rather than expecting benefits by default.

2025 · Effect of generative artificial intelligence on university students learning outcomes: A systematic review and meta-analysis

Educational Research Review (Elsevier, doi 10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100737) · meta-analysis, 57 studies / 97 effect sizes, 5,389 university students, AT-MCSCL framework, random effects (I²=93.1%)
  • Raw overall effect is large: g+=0.804 — but trim-and-fill correction for publication bias drops it to g+=0.321 (Egger intercept 2.975, p=0.002); academic achievement 0.633→0.354 adjusted.
  • By outcome: language skills g+=2.331 (heavily biased), academic achievement 0.633, affective-motivational 0.617, higher-order thinking 0.580, metacognition n.s. (g+=0.078) — no evidence GenAI builds metacognition on its own.
  • Effects largest for freshmen (0.698), language disciplines (1.782) vs STEM (0.578); medium samples 40–100 give 1.218 vs large samples >100 only 0.465; classroom g+=0.891 vs laboratory 0.261; 4–12-week interventions 1.014 vs >12 weeks 0.350 (n.s.).
  • Context equity signal: low-ICT countries 1.764, low-SES students 1.751, high power-distance 0.859 (significant) vs low power-distance 0.236 (n.s.); South/Southeast Asia g+=0.651.
DDCT: L6 with the sharpest caveat in this folder (bias-adjusted truth ≈ g 0.32, not 0.80) — grounds S0's "defend the fundamentals" argument in real numbers; classroom-embedded beats lab-pilot (test any DDCT AI block inside real studio courses, not sandbox demos); SE-Asia/high-power-distance context suggests Thai students respond strongly to structured, teacher-framed AI use.

2025 · Impact of generative AI interaction and output quality on university students’ learning outcomes: a technology-mediated and motivation-driven approach

Scientific Reports (Nature portfolio) · two-wave longitudinal survey (4 months apart) + CB-SEM and latent moderated SEM · n=323 Chinese undergraduates (58.4% engineering; 68.4% ChatGPT users)
  • GAI output quality directly predicts learning outcomes (β=0.405 wave 1; β=0.346 wave 2, p<0.001; total effect 0.538 = 0.453 direct + 0.085 via motivation) — mere interaction has no direct path to outcomes, only indirect ones via learning motivation (β=0.116–0.148) and creative self-efficacy (β=0.179–0.189).
  • Motivational chain replicates in both waves: learning motivation → academic self-efficacy (β=0.421–0.552) → creative self-efficacy (β=0.373–0.428) → outcomes (β=0.200–0.143).
  • Creative thinking moderation flips over time: strongly negative in wave 1 (β=−136.322, p<0.001 — outlier-scale value, interpret direction only) but positive in wave 2 (β=0.182, p<0.05): creative students first find AI interaction disappointing, then productive after familiarization.
  • Self-reported scale study — outcomes are perceptions ("using GAI improved my learning"), not grades; single-country sample.
DDCT: L1 (aligned AI beats raw AI — here: output quality, not usage frequency, is the active ingredient) + AI-as-junior-staff loop — the brief→draft→critique/quality-gate step is exactly the mechanism this paper says carries the effect; also predicts DD art-track "creative" students may need a deliberate familiarization phase before AI blocks pay off (S4 conversion design).

2025 · The effectiveness of ChatGPT in assisting high school students in programming learning: evidence from a quasi-experimental research

Interactive Learning Environments 33(6):3726–3743 (Taylor & Francis) · quasi-experiment + post-hoc interviews · n=153 students (77 ChatGPT / 76 conventional), girls’ senior high school, Taiwan; 6 weeks C++ (3-week common baseline + 3-week treatment); MANCOVA/ANCOVA
  • Honest negative: the ChatGPT group scored significantly lower on all three outcomes — flow experience (F=9.20, p=0.003, η²=0.06), self-efficacy (F=15.78, p<0.001, η²=0.10), and learning achievement (F=3.96, p=0.048, η²=0.03; posttest 42.42 vs 50.52).
  • Interview causes: communication overhead ("I had to repeat requests constantly"), answers not adapted to the learner’s level, and attribution of success to the AI undermining self-efficacy ("Though it gave me direct answers, I lacked the process of thinking and organizing myself"); 6/11 interviewees found it helpful, 5 did not.
  • Remedy proposed — the guidance–practice–transformation (G–P–T) model: (1) guided keywords/prompts, (2) practice assignments interspersed with bi-directional annotation (student and ChatGPT annotate each other’s code) as a comprehension gate, (3) reflective transformation tasks (report on one’s own project).
  • Instructional setting was conventional lecture-based; ChatGPT used as a virtual TA for syntax/debugging — i.e., raw, unstructured access for novices.
DDCT: the folder’s strongest anti-“just give them ChatGPT” evidence (L2 integration mode) + DR7/DR27 (student identity & anxiety — forcing AI-skeptical or novice students onto raw AI hurts self-efficacy, the exact risk in DDCT’s population); copy G–P–T’s bi-directional annotation exercise as a 🟡-zone assignment in S0/S4 intro blocks.

2025 · The impact of GenAI on learning outcomes: A systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental studies

Elsevier-format review article (venue not printed in converted text) · PRISMA meta-analysis, 68 studies / 337 effect sizes (2022–2025), SMD, random effects (I²=95%); East China Normal University
  • Overall effect moderate: SMD=0.45 (95% CI 0.43–0.47); distribution is wide — 35% of effect sizes ≥0.8 while 29% <0.2; fail-safe N=4,882 (robust to unpublished nulls).
  • By dimension: behavioral engagement SMD=1.08 and self-regulation/metacognition 0.74 (monitoring & reflection 0.77) beat cognitive development 0.36 and affective-motivational 0.38 (emotional states n.s. 0.03).
  • Inside cognition, higher-order thinking (0.36) ≈ basic knowledge acquisition (0.34; between-group p=.55) — no premium for depth.
  • Moderators: natural sciences 0.70 > humanities 0.46 > social sciences 0.39; secondary 0.58 > university 0.41; short-term 0.49 > long-term 0.35; medium/small samples 0.55/0.52 vs large samples 0.33.
DDCT: L6 (higher-order ≈ basic → GenAI is not a HOT machine) + L3 (reflection scaffolds: monitoring/reflection is the biggest measurable winner — build the judgment notebook into AI blocks) — contradicts papers 3–4 on duration (here short > long), reinforcing that "how" dominates "how long" (L2).

2026 · Generative AI in higher education: A systematic review of empirical uses, outcomes, and risks

International Journal of Educational Research Open (Elsevier, doi 10.1016/j.ijedro.2026.100584) · PRISMA 2020 systematic review, 103 empirical studies (Jan 2023–Oct 2025), content analysis into 7 use categories + 4-level outcome typology, inter-coder κ=0.781
  • 56% of studies are "positive but conditional"; the stable explanatory lens across domains is scaffolding vs substitution — effects depend on pedagogy, assessment design, and verification, not on the model.
  • Concrete positive cases: custom GPT-4 tutor produced higher physics learning gains in less time than in-class active learning (Kestin et al.); guidance-based ChatGPT aid that requires an initial attempt then gives hints (not answers) beat standard ChatGPT on self-regulated learning and higher-order thinking (Lee et al.; Makransky et al.); teacher+AI interactive feedback produced the largest writing gains while passive AI-only feedback produced the smallest (Jiang); rubric-prompted LLM grading matched instructor scores on Python assignments and "dramatically reduced grading time" (Bernik).
  • Neutral-by-design: Kosar-style courses "absorb" GenAI via tool-free exams, oral defenses, in-lab extensions, localized tasks — neutral grades = successful risk-managed integration, not failure.
  • Risks quantified/observed: heavy LLM use for code generation & debugging linked to lower final grades while explanation-seeking is not (Jošt); Turnitin flagged AI submissions but staff reported only ~half of suspected cases and AI work earned similar grades (Perkins) — detection is brittle; higher GenAI dependency linked to lower GPA (Revesai); high performers use AI for disciplined autonomy while weaker students substitute → gaps widen.
  • Greenspan randomized field experiment: GenAI-permitted sections improved attitudes, prohibited sections raised grades — acceptance ≠ achievement.
DDCT: the folder’s case catalog — L1/L2/L5 + DR23 + L7 (faculty training works: Oman lecturer capacity-building improved GenAI teaching use, Alsaiari); mine it for the attempt-first tutor pattern, hybrid AI+teacher feedback, rubric-prompted grading for studio-scale feedback, and the "explanation-seeking is safe, solution-generation erodes grades" rule to teach students explicitly.

2026 · Impact of ChatGPT on learning outcomes and performance of students in computer programming courses: a mixed-method approach

Open-access journal (venue not printed in converted text) · mixed-methods: student survey + instructor survey + course records · N=855 students (from 1,889 enrolled) in a mandatory undergraduate programming course, Nigeria + 17 markers/instructors; correlation + OLS regression
  • 98.4% of students had prior ChatGPT exposure; affective ratings were positive: enjoyment 3.93/5, motivation 3.75/5, confidence 3.72/5, usefulness 2.56/3.
  • But perceptions did not translate into performance: regression on final scores R²=0.006 (0.6% of variance), no predictor significant (all p>0.1); mean score 7.21/10 (SD 3.57).
  • Correlates cluster only among perceptions (concept understanding ↔ problem-solving r=0.640; error-fixing ↔ concepts r=0.577; reliance ↔ assignment completion r=0.572) — a closed loop of beliefs, not outcomes.
  • Markers (n=17): students rely on ChatGPT solutions without fully understanding them (median 4/5), noticeable code similarity across students (3.94/5), changed problem-solving approaches since ChatGPT (3.77/5).
DDCT: large-sample confirmation of the perception–performance decoupling (L6) + DR23 — "students like it" is not an outcome argument for curriculum change; marker-observed solution-copying supports mandatory process evidence (drafts, versions, defense) rather than polished-artifact grading.

2026 · Rewriting the curriculum: A systematic review of generative AI-driven pedagogical change and emerging systems of learning in higher education

Elsevier-format article (venue not printed in converted text) · triangulated mixed methods: scoping review + VOSviewer bibliometrics (n=209 records) + SLR of 36 studies (2023–2025) + 10 semi-structured interviews with academic leaders across 5 institutions in Australia & Indonesia; proposes the "AI-Augmented Learning System" framework
  • "Curricula" is only a peripheral node in the co-occurrence map — curriculum-level transformation is marginal in the literature; capabilities & skills explicitly addressed in just 3/36 articles.
  • Five system shifts: static curriculum → dynamic AI-integrated design; teacher-centred delivery → AI-augmented facilitation; knowledge transfer → capability development; local experimentation → institutional governance; fragmented use → ecosystemic integration. Via SAMR, most current practice sits at substitution/augmentation — genuine redefinition is rare.
  • Concrete practitioner cases: an Australian Go8 college runs a faculty-level "traffic signal" AI policy ("restrict / allow with guardrails / totally unrestricted" applied college-wide) plus a mandatory staff integrity module; one educator requires students to submit a disclosure table listing every AI tool used and how; another institution gives all students a free GenAI tool to prevent a digital divide.
  • Reform reported as "incremental and unit-led rather than institution-wide"; decentralized tool-building creates integration costs ("we created our own system … it’s actually more troublesome").
DDCT: direct external validation of S0 — the traffic-signal policy is a live instance of the 3-zone (🟢🟡🔴) policy and DR16 disclosure (the disclosure-table assignment is a ready-made 🟡-zone artifact); DR24/DR25 (faculty readiness, slow/fast layers: continuous check-up points on how people actually use AI); DR26 DigiPen channel: coordinate institution-wide rather than per-program to avoid the Indonesian fragmentation trap.

Top 3 cases for the DDCT curriculum

1. The "Maribor package" — assessment redesign that safely absorbs ChatGPT (Kosar et al. 2024, corroborated across 6 studies in Hawi & Samaha 2026)

What was done: AI-resistant assignment formats (UML diagrams, provided code/main, minimal text) + weekly in-lab extension problems solved without AI + interactive oral defense (explain/change/complete your code) + paper-based midterms. · Measured result: across n=182 over a full semester, ChatGPT vs no-ChatGPT showed no difference in labs (p=0.760), midterms (p=0.856), or final grade (p=0.730) — permission became harmless; usage shifted from code generation to optimization/comparison, and only 21/69 opted to use it on every assignment. · Strengthens: S0 3-zone policy + DR23 + L5 — DDCT can run 🟢 AI-permitted homework precisely because 🔴 AI-free defenses/exams anchor grades to internalized competence; refresh formats each year as models improve.

2. Attempt-first, hints-not-answers AI tutor wrappers (Lee et al. 2024, Makransky et al. 2025, Kestin et al. 2025; programming variant: G–P–T model, Yang et al. 2025)

What was done: require an initial student attempt, then deploy GenAI for structured hints/teach-back/guided questioning rather than solutions; in physics (Kestin) a custom GPT-4 tutor; in chemistry (Lee/Makransky) a guidance-based ChatGPT aid vs standard ChatGPT; G–P–T adds bi-directional annotation (student and ChatGPT annotate each other’s code) as a comprehension gate. · Measured result: higher learning gains in less time than in-class active learning (Kestin); better self-regulated learning and higher-order thinking than generic ChatGPT (Lee); stronger conceptual knowledge at key timepoints (Makransky) — vs Yang’s raw-access control group, which lost achievement, flow, and self-efficacy. · Strengthens: L1/L3 + S1 (AI-Augmented Production track) + S5 (L2 applied blocks) + AI-as-junior-staff loop — the brief→draft→critique→quality-gate cycle is the studio-scale version of attempt-first tutoring; expect AI-skeptical DD students to need a guided familiarization phase before gains appear (Bai & Wang wave-1 negative → wave-2 positive moderation).

3. Counterbalanced AI-on/AI-off lab blocks with a published multidimensional quality rubric (Andleeb et al. 2025)

What was done: two-group counterbalanced design — each student cohort alternated ChatGPT and no-ChatGPT across consecutive assignments, scored with a replicable rubric (correctness, efficiency, code quality, warning analysis, error handling; κ=0.87) plus timed work and post-task concept checks. · Measured result: rubric scores significantly higher with AI both times (t=4.46 p=0.018; t=5.02 p<0.001; error handling 2.0 vs 0.05), completion time cut 2.2–4.8× (p≤0.0011), conceptual gains mixed (n.s. on functions; U=77.0 p=0.0457 on structures). · Strengthens: S0 3-week tool-sprint blocks + DR23 + L2 — the evaluation pattern itself is the transferable artifact: alternate 🟢/🔴 weeks inside a block so students experience and measure the delta (speed & polish up, concepts unchanged), and reuse the published rubric for QC-style grading that maps onto DR9/DR14 pipeline-quality habits.

Gaps & notes

  • Meta-analytic spread is wide and partly contradictory. Overall effects range SMD 0.45 (Han) to g 0.609 (Zhao) to g+ 0.804 (Chen & Cheung — but 0.321 after trim-and-fill); duration findings conflict (Zhao: 8–16-week peak; Chen: 4–12-week peak; Han: short > long). Common safe reading: gains are real but modest, context-dominated, and inflated by publication bias in achievement/language outcomes.
  • Metacognition contradiction: Chen & Cheung find no significant metacognition effect (g+=0.078) while Han reports self-regulation/metacognition SMD=0.74 — different constructs/measures; DDCT should not assume AI builds metacognition without explicit reflection scaffolds.
  • Programming-specific nulls cluster: Kosar (null), Jošt/Cubillos/Sun (no grade differences, via Hawi & Samaha), Gambo (R²=0.006, n=855) vs positive lab effects at small n (Andleeb, n=27). Large-sample and semester-long designs trend null; short controlled tasks trend positive on speed/quality, not concepts.
  • Honest negatives are use-conditioned: the high-school decrement (Yang) and the usage-mode findings (code-generation/debugging reliance → lower grades; explanation-seeking → neutral) all point to unstructured novice access as the failure mode, consistent with L2.
  • Venue gaps: three converted texts (Han et al.; Gambo et al.; Sabani et al.) do not print journal names in the source markdown — citations given as far as the text allows. Numbers not present in the texts are marked "not reported" rather than estimated.
  • Thin coverage for DDCT’s art/design side: creativity has the weakest HOT effect (g=0.444, Zhao) and visual-tool effects are the smallest content type (g+=0.510, Chen) — no experimental evidence yet on generative image/video/3D pipelines in art-school settings; folder 1 supports the pedagogy layer, not the media layer.
  • Practitioner-level policies lack measurement: the traffic-light policy and disclosure table (Sabani) are interview evidence — directly analogous to DDCT’s 3-zone design but with no outcome numbers yet.
