2 · Top cases across all 9 folders (shortlist)

1. DDDOS — per-stage AI-gating sheet inside one brief

What: Double-Diamond operation sheet naming which AI tool is allowed at which stage + self-monitoring questions (image-gen banned during Define). Measured: achievement 20.90 vs 18.65 (GAI-only) vs 16.13 (traditional), p<.001; self-efficacy 4.25/3.84/3.54 (n=120×3). Folder 4 → S0 3-zone operationalized per assignment (DR15/DR23).
📄 Paper: Song & Xu 2026 — Double Diamond design thinking strategy in GAI-supported design learning

2. Student-trained LoRA customization lab

What: each student trains a personal LoRA (20 images, ~40 min, one RTX 4060 Ti) inside a 6-week poster course with sketch-before-AI gate. Measured: engagement Δ cognitive +0.472, emotional +0.396, behavioural +0.286 (all p<.001); anxiety shifts from output to configuration; 2.1:1 positive emotions (RCT n=96). Folder 4 → S0 local-model lab (DR11 NDA-safe), DR22 Thai-culture LoRAs as differentiating assets.
📄 Paper: Li, Sun & Kim — Effects of Customized Generative AI on Student Engagement and Emotions (Visual Communication Design)

3. CS50 guardrailed tutor + human-feedback fine-tune loop

What: RAG over course content, guardrails, throttling; then 50-conversation fine-tune judged by arena evals. Measured: 88% vs 48% curricular accuracy, 86% approval, $1.90/student/mo at 1.8M queries; eval-human agreement 82%. Folder 2 → a DDCT "studio duck" over DigiPen content (S0, L1/L5).
📄 Papers: Teaching CS50 with AI (2024) · Improving AI in CS50 — Leveraging Human Feedback (2025)

4. Lehigh AI-literacy course architecture

What: 13 weeks over an authentic workflow; AI-use log + attribution template on every submission; authorial-mastery viva; hallucination theory taught after students get burned. Measured: attribution d=+2.40, hallucination-detection d=+1.45, 88% transfer intent (n=26). Folder 3 → S0 AI-Fluency core blueprint (swap spine to DD/CT production pipeline).
📄 Paper: A Discipline-Agnostic AI Literacy Course for Academic Research

5. AIAS 5-level scale with mechanics, not labels

What: per-brief levels requiring prompt rationales, storyboard artefacts, staged checkpoints; "relabeling ≠ redesign". Measured: 300+ institutions, 30 translations, TEQSA-recognized; storyboard = 25% of grade at Level 2. Folder 3 → grading grammar for every DDCT brief; subdivide 🟡 into Levels 2–4 (DR23/DR28/DR12).
📄 Papers: The AI Assessment Scale Revisited (2024) · Reimagining the AI Assessment Scale (2025)

6. Klapproth 3-column AI-use protocol + unaided bookends

What: document (my start | AI contribution | justified adopt/modify/reject), graded on decision quality; every AI sequence closes with an unaided parallel task; AI rule derived from the ILO. Measured: backed by retention 57.5% vs 68.5% (d=0.68) and failure 2–6%→18.4%. Folder 3 → the judgment notebook, operationalized (DR23/L2/L3/L6).
📄 Paper: Redesigning Teaching, Learning, and Assessment in the Age of Generative AI — A Constructive Alignment Perspective

7. Four-pillar AI-resilient assessment kit

What: rubric weighting reasoning 25–35% / oral defense 25–35% / process 20–30% / product ≤20%; 8–12 min viva protocol with workload math (17–35 h per 100 students; 30–40% sampling); Prohibited/Guided/Permitted taxonomy; 6-question vulnerability audit. Folder 8 → S0/S4 capstone instrument, pilot first (DR23).
📄 Paper: Designing AI-Resilient Assessment — Four-Pillar Framework

8. FeedbackWriter — TA-gated AI critique at scale

What: rubric-anchored GPT-4o suggestions, empty-by-default boxes, TA adopt/edit/dismiss before students see anything. Measured: revision quality d=0.50 (50th→70th percentile), 88.7% TA approval of 24,270 judgments (RCT N=354). Folder 8 → studio critique + capstone feedback scaling (L1/L2, DR14); caution: weaker students get weaker AI feedback (Matthew effect, folder 8).
📄 Paper: AI-Mediated Feedback Improves Student Revisions — RCT FeedbackWriter (CHI'26)

9. Three-integration-mode quasi-experiment (book design)

What: traditional vs ideation-chatbot vs full-workflow AIGC in one real course. Measured: full 85.34 > traditional 74.98 ≈ chatbot 76.37 (η²p=.285); chatbot arm lowest self-efficacy (33.54) and role clarity; 10/10 students reframed designer as "art decision-maker" (n=100). Folder 4 → the L2/DR27 case against chatbot-sprinkling for AI-skeptical cohorts.
📄 Paper: Exploring AI-enhanced design education in China — quasi-experimental study of AIGC conditions

10. Maribor package — safe permission

What: AI-resistant briefs + AI-free in-lab extensions + oral defense + paper exams with unrestricted ChatGPT homework. Measured: labs p=0.760, midterm p=0.856, final p=0.730 — null differences (n=182); usage self-regulated (21/69 weekly). Folder 1 → S0 defense-zone design (L5/DR23).
📄 Paper: Kosar et al. — Computer Science Education in ChatGPT Era (Mathematics 2024)

11. XJTLU gamified asynchronous AI-literacy course

What: level-up points + leaderboard + peer-rated collaborative argumentation, 13 weeks. Measured: 4,898 enrolled; cognitive 5.05→5.44, self-efficacy 5.07→5.50 (p<.001) — but ethics null (p=0.09). Folder 9 → S0 fluency-course chassis; route ethics to facilitated studio cases (L4), use affective gains to convert AI-hostile students (DR27).
📄 Paper: Game-Based Learning for Asynchronous AI Literacy Course (European J. of Education 2026)

12. AI-fills-the-artist-seat game jam + survey instrument

What: jam configuration change (GenAI replaces the artist seat) + 19-question survey. Measured: 45.9% substantial learning gains, 56.7% reuse intent; AI asset reliability 2.16/5, 48.6% "slightly worse than human"; backgrounds/menus work, sprites/sheets fail (n=37). Folder 5 → honest pipeline placement (DR1/DR9) and a reusable baseline instrument for DDCT's own jam pilots (S4/S5-L3, L8).
📄 Paper: Evaluating Generative AI in Game Asset Creation through a Game Jam (DiGRA)

13. 20-hour AI retrofit + dedicated technical instructor

What: bolt-on 20 class-hours of AI skills + ethics threads inside existing crits; 1 technical instructor per 1:9 studio; university cloud SD/ComfyUI lab. Measured: 95.7% ≥ moderately beneficial, ethics-in-crit preferred 2:1 — but only 5.9% became frequent users (n=70). Folder 6 → the cheapest S0 studio retrofit; pair with phase-sensitive scaffolding (L4/L7/DR12).
📄 Paper: Teaching with AI in Architecture — Core Design Studio (Buildings 2025)

14. ChemE 8-prompt course-rebuild sprint

What: 3 faculty × 3 courses rebuilt via a standardized 8-prompt ChatGPT-4o protocol. Measured: syllabus/ABET-map = clear time savings, assessments = net time loss + conceptual errors; students 87–92% adoption but trust 2.65–2.87/5 and explicit demand for AI rules (n=78). Folder 7 → faculty redesign sprint for S0 (L7) + student rule-demand validates the 3-zone policy.
📄 Paper: Generative AI in Chemical Engineering Education (ASEE 2025)

15. Brain-first sequencing, grade-weighted 80/10/10

What: unaided draft (80% of grade, human feedback) → AI revision (10%) → reflection (10%). Measured: perception improved 67.86%, help-seeking fell 45.16% (caveat); MIT EEG crossover: unaided-first→AI-later preserves ownership and connectivity (83.3% vs 11.1% quote failure). Folders 8+1 → default assignment skeleton for both DD and CT (DR23/DR3/L6).
📄 Papers: Examining AI-assisted writing revision through assignment design · Your Brain on ChatGPT — Cognitive Debt (MIT, preprint)

3 · Prioritized reading list for the working group

Ordered by decision-relevance to the DDCT curriculum work, not by academic prestige. Tier 1 ≈ one sitting each; every entry links to the source PDF.

Tier 1 · Read before drafting policy & assessment (5)

Song & Xu 2026 — Double Diamond design thinking strategy in GAI-supported design learning
The per-brief AI-gating instrument (DDDOS) with the library's cleanest 3-arm result (20.90 / 18.65 / 16.13, p<.001). Copy-adapt the stage/tool matrix for any DD or CT studio brief.

Klapproth 2026 — Redesigning Teaching, Learning, and Assessment (Constructive Alignment)
The 3-column AI-use protocol + close-every-AI-sequence-with-an-unaided-task rule + "AI rule derives from the ILO". The judgment notebook, operationalized, with the retention/failure numbers behind it.
3
Nikolić & Basta Nikolić 2026 — Designing AI-Resilient Assessment: Four-Pillar Framework
Ready-to-adopt capstone kit: weighted rubric (product ≤20%), costed viva protocol with sampling math, AI-permission taxonomy, 6-question vulnerability audit. Pilot before scaling.
4
2026 — A Discipline-Agnostic AI Literacy Course for Academic Research (Lehigh)
The S0 AI-Fluency core blueprint: 13-week workflow spine, AI-use log + attribution template, authorial-mastery viva, hallucination theory after the burn (d=+2.40 attribution). Swap the spine to a DD/CT production pipeline.

Li & Chen 2026 — Exploring AI-enhanced design education in China (3 AIGC conditions)
The integration-mode evidence for AI-skeptical cohorts: full-workflow wins, chatbot-sprinkle is no better than traditional and worst for self-efficacy. Decides the "how," not just "whether," of AI in DD studios.

Tier 3 · Evidence backbone — cite selectively (6)
13
Chiang 2026 — Effectiveness of GenAI in design pedagogy: meta-analysis of usage modes
N=2,847, d=0.60 overall; instructor–student collaboration d=0.89 > student-led 0.58 > instructor-led 0.51 — the L1/L2/L3 backbone for studio integration choices.
14
Chen & Cheung 2025 — Effect of GenAI on university students' learning outcomes (meta)
The bias-correction reality check: raw g=0.804 → 0.321 after trim-and-fill; classroom 0.891 vs lab 0.261 — test AI blocks inside real courses and set expectations accordingly.
15
2026 — How AI Assistance Impacts the Formation of Coding Skills (Anthropic RCT)
The atrophy caution for CT: AI access cost 17% of learning (d=0.738) — unless students use conceptual-inquiry patterns (65–86% vs 24–39%). Keep 🔴 formation blocks; grade the inquiry patterns.
16
Gerlich 2025 — AI Tools in Society: Cognitive Offloading and Critical Thinking
The canonical offloading citation (r=−0.68 with critical thinking) behind the Brake·Anchor·Engine counterweight — correlational; pair with the self-efficacy mediation nuance from folder 8.
17
Kosmyna et al. 2025 — Your Brain on ChatGPT (MIT Media Lab, preprint)
EEG cognitive-debt evidence for brain-first sequencing (83.3% couldn't quote their own essay). Compelling but non-peer-reviewed, n=18/group — cite with the caveat.
18
2026 — Who Wrote This? Reliability of AI Detection Tools in Higher Education
Preregistered detector reality check (45.5% of real theses flagged, mean 34% AI; hybrids hardest) — the evidence for vivas + process logs instead of detection-as-verdict.

5 · Gaps & honest caveats

  • Generative media pipeline evidence is thin: creativity is the weakest higher-order gain (g=0.444); visual tools weakest content type (0.510); no experimental evidence for generative image/video/3D in art-school pipelines — DDCT's core domain is precisely the frontier (folders 1, 4).
  • Nothing empirical on IP fog or NDA-safe models (DR10–12) beyond the LoRA-lab case; detection research is text-only — no visual/3D detection (folder 8).
  • No study measures beyond ~16 weeks; Bayesian meta found zero delayed-posttest designs (folder 2) — DDCT should instrument its own delayed measures.
  • Detector rankings disagree across studies (model/corpus snapshots); MIT cognitive-debt is a non-peer-reviewed preprint (n=18/group) — cite with caveats.
  • Thai/SEA context nearly absent: SE-Asia meta subgroups rest on k=4; China dominates the quasi-experimental design-education evidence (folders 1, 3, 4).
  • Known library misses: 5 🔒 paywalled + 3 🤖 one-click (see MANIFEST.html) + 1 wrong-source PDF (folder 4 "DRS 2024 China AIGC" is actually DRS's 9-page ethics statement — re-download the paper; digest card flags it).
