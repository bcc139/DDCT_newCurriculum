2023 (file labeled 2024) — Generative AI tools in art education: Exploring prompt engineering and iterative processes for enhanced creativity

Metaverse 4(1) · Hutson & Cotroneo · mixed-methods studio case study, n=15 undergrads (Digital Art II–III, US liberal-arts university), Spring 2023, DALL-E 2
  • 60% wanted AI exercises in artmaking, but 66.67% said the tools did not enhance their final work — value concentrated in ideation/sketchbook stages.
  • 73.33% better understood how prompt input shapes output; prompt iterations were heavy: only 20% needed 1–3, while 13.33% needed >20.
  • Most common prompt modification: extending description (26.47% of responses); least common: changing artist style (2.94%). Continued-use intent weak: 46.67% unsure, 20% said no.
  • 10 of 14 discussion posts called AI "a useful tool for generating ideas"; students raised copyright/job fears but none could cite evidence of artists being replaced.
DDCT: S0 tool-sprint design + L3 — copy the assignment trio: (a) 10 prompts/≥4 variations with Synectic triggers, (b) recreate an AI image from scratch in Photoshop (craft counterweight / Brake·Anchor·Engine), (c) Digital Art III peer loop: draw alien plant → peer writes text description → peer's prompt visualizes it (trains critique vocabulary, Direction Ladder taste→critique). DR7: mixed continuation intent = design sprints to change minds, not assume conversion.

2025 — Exploring the Integration of Generative AI into Art and Design Education Practice

ISAIE 2025 (ACM) · Zhang, Liu, Huang, Lou, Lu, Shanghai DianJi University · pre/post classroom experiment, n=102 (4 cohorts, Digital Media Art + Industrial Design Engineering), 3-hr "Protect the Ocean" poster task, pre-test Adobe vs post-test DeepSeek+Dream AI, 3 blind judges
  • Output volume rose 114 → 245 works (+114.91%); Digital Media Art Y2 gained most (+153.57%, 28→71 works).
  • Quality: mean expert score 7.16 → 7.86 (+0.71/10); the strongest gain came from Industrial Design graduate Y1 (+1.01), the weakest from DMA Y3 (+0.43) — students with stronger foundations extracted more quality from AI (L1: craft base beats raw tool).
  • GERR "critical integration" framework: Generation → Evaluation (5-criteria matrix incl. ethical compliance) → Reconstruction (hand-drawn/cultural re-personalization) → Reflection ("AI solution modification logs": why option A over B).
  • Policy: direct submission of raw AI output treated as academic misconduct; AI-use statements + copyright self-check forms required.
DDCT: L2/L3 + DR23 — the GERR loop and "why A over B" modification log is a ready-made judgment-notebook format; the AI-use statement + self-check form is a deployable 3-zone (🟡 disclosed) artifact for S0. Caveat: single-group pre/post, 3 judges, no counterfactual.

2025 — The analysis of generative artificial intelligence technology for innovative thinking and strategies in animation teaching

Open-access article (Scientific Reports style) · Xu, Zhong, Cao (Communication Univ. of Zhejiang et al.) · controlled mixed-methods experiment, 120 students, 12-week GAI platform (plus 8- and 10-week sub-experiments), exp vs control
  • Basic knowledge 85.2 vs 78.5 (t=4.92, p<.001, d=0.99); application skills 81.4 vs 72.1; learning efficiency +21.4%, motivation +25%, engagement +30%.
  • Creation quality 84.2 vs 76.4 (η²=0.22); creation time 4.1h vs 5.2h (η²=0.31); teamwork 85.6 vs 74.1 (d=1.20); problem-solving 81.2 vs 70.3 (d=1.38).
  • Stratified: basic tasks got −24.5% completion time (AI automates in-betweens); advanced tasks got creativity +10.6 pts and collaboration 4.5 vs 2.9 — AI helps differently by task tier.
  • Proficiency split: low-ability students gained most knowledge (+32.5 vs +15.3 control); high-ability gained most creative expression (+31.2 vs +9.6).
  • Resource note: RunwayML/cloud APIs ran on ordinary PCs/iPads; proposes distilled offline models for low-infrastructure settings.
DDCT: L1 + DR13 durable-vs-churn — the task-tier finding (efficiency at basic tier, collaboration/judgment gains at advanced tier) is the empirical basis for splitting AI use by course level in S1's Y3 fork; cloud-API fallback supports S0's lab on modest hardware. ⚠ Internal inconsistencies (group n's/df's vs stated 120; 12/8/10-week arms) — cite numbers with caution.

2025 — Generative Artificial Intelligence as a Catalyst for Change in Higher Education Art Study Programs

Computers 14(4):154 (MDPI) · Ansone, Zalīte-Supe, Daniela (Univ. of Latvia) · observational case study, n=10 bachelor art students, same composition task done traditionally (1.5h) then with DALL-E (30-min think-aloud) + questionnaire
  • 7/10 found AI most useful for brainstorming/ideation, not fine composition; all 10 iterated prompts 3–6 times; 7/10 achieved only partial alignment with artistic vision, 2 failed to reach it.
  • Control failures: 7 struggled with spatial placement/proportions; 6 hit spurious extra elements; 4 couldn't hold a fixed color scheme; 3 tried seed values, still no exact replication.
  • Ratings: ideation quality 4.5/5, productivity 4.3/5, ease-of-use only 3.8/5; 8/10 called AI complementary not a replacement; 7/10 intend to keep using it.
  • Asks: hands-on workshops (4 students), AI case studies (3), collaborative AI projects (2); rubrics separating legitimate AI collaboration from shortcuts.
DDCT: DR9 control techniques + DR23 — the "same brief, traditional then AI" paired task is a cheap, transferable assessment design that lets faculty see exactly where AI breaks down (spatial/numeric/color control); feeds the 🔴 AI-free and 🟡 zones with evidence. Well-structured prompts reportedly lift accuracy 85%→98% (cited claim, not measured here).

2025 — Research on AIGC-Integrated Design Education for Sustainable Teaching: TAM + TPACK empirical analysis

Sustainability 17:5497 (MDPI) · Huang, Fu, Zhao (Hanyang Univ.) · mixed methods: 10-university course audit (2022–24), surveys (52 teachers, 219 students), 20 interviews, OLS path model
  • Course audit: ~70% of courses set creative-thinking (not tool) objectives; ~50% use "module-embedded" AI, ~30% demo-only, only 1/10 full-process embedding; ~90% workshop+lecture hybrid; most still outcome-only assessment.
  • Students: creative activation M=4.28 (highest), efficiency 4.22, continued-use intent 4.21; lower — output control 3.56, ethical awareness 3.32 (SD>0.9); >38% struggle to optimize prompts (confidence M=2.96).
  • Teachers: TK→CK significant (β=0.26, p=.007; R²=0.32) but PK→CK not (β=0.07, p=.56) — pedagogy hasn't caught up with tools; 42% of teachers "not confident" guiding student AI use; only 21% teach AI ethics; pedagogical-strategy items M=3.19.
  • Pattern named: "technology-driven → methodologically lagging → content-reconstructing"; "having tools but lacking methodology."

2026 — A collaborative creation framework for experimental animation pedagogy integrating GAI and vibe coding

Frontiers in Psychology 17:1940505 · Mao (Soochow University) · design-based research, 5-week Experimental Animation course, n=40 3rd-year Digital Media Art undergrads (no prior AI/vibe-coding), 3-wave survey (AI-partner perception + Leppink cognitive load)
  • AI-partner perception 3.30 → 4.09 (t=−4.18, p<.001, d=0.66); extraneous load 3.91 → 2.90 (d≈1.0, p<.001); germane load 3.38 → 3.97 (p=.009, d=0.43); intrinsic load unchanged — technical barriers fell while deep-processing effort rose.
  • All change occurred in the second half of the course (pre→mid n.s.): the partner relationship is built by structured tasks, not by tool exposure.
  • Vibe coding (Cursor IDE) entered at week 3 to break the motion bottleneck: prompts like "loop/breathe" were flattened to zoom/pan by image generators; code-driven kinetic logic restored non-anthropocentric motion ("how to program a kinetic logic" vs "how to animate").
  • AI role trajectory: tool → assistant → exploratory resource → creative collaborator; final week requires a written human-AI collaboration statement (authorship disclosure).
DDCT: S0/S4 flagship ingredient — a 5-week "generation-feedback-evolution" block with GAI for concept + vibe coding for motion, measured to reduce extraneous load while raising partner perception (directly counters AI-skeptic anxiety, DR27/L8). DR16: collaboration statements = disclosure practice; DR9: honest pipeline placement (image model ≠ motion system). No control group (within-group design).

2026 — Effects of integrating a structured design thinking strategy into GAI-supported design learning (Double Diamond)

Frontiers in Psychology 17:1847432 · Song & Xu (Universiti Sains Malaysia + China) · true experiment, 120 undergrads ×3 conditions (n=40 each) in a 4-week Card Game Character Design course: GAI+Double-Diamond sheet vs GAI-only vs traditional
  • Design achievement: 20.90 vs 18.65 vs 16.13 (F=43.28, p<.001; all pairwise significant: GAI-SDTS > GAI > TDL); rubric by 2 raters, Kendall's W=0.94.
  • Creative self-efficacy (ANCOVA-adjusted): 4.25 vs 3.84 vs 3.54 (F=803.16, p<.001); problem-solving: 4.44 vs 4.04 vs 3.71 (F=461.14, p<.001); personal-control sub-scale gap largest (4.11 vs 3.29 vs 2.75).
  • The intervention artifact: a Double-Diamond Design Operation Sheet (DDDOS) — stage objectives, self-monitoring questions, which AI tool is allowed at which stage (DeepSeek for Discover/Define, Dreamina for Develop/Deliver, Dreamina deliberately blocked in Define to avoid premature visual fixation), stage-completion checks.
  • Author caveat: because both GAI groups generated/refined visuals with AI, scores partly reflect AI artifact quality, not independent ability.
DDCT: L1 in its cleanest experimental form — scaffolding beats raw tool access beats no AI; the DDDOS is a copy-ready template for S0's AI-Fluency core and 3-week sprint blocks (its per-stage tool gating literally instantiates the 3-zone policy inside one assignment). ⚠ ANCOVA F values are unusually large with tiny SDs — replicate before quoting externally.

2026 — Effectiveness of generative AI in higher education design pedagogy: a meta-analysis of usage modes

International Journal of Technology and Design Education (Springer) · Chiang (National Yunlin Univ. of Science and Technology, Taiwan) · PRISMA meta-analysis, 27 controlled studies (9 RCT + 18 quasi-exp), N=2,847, 2023–2026, 14 countries; DerSimonian-Laird random effects
  • Overall effect d=0.60 (95% CI [0.55, 0.65]), low heterogeneity I²=21.0%; leave-one-out range d=0.589–0.607.
  • Usage mode significant (Q=8.94, p=.011): instructor–student collaboration d=0.89 (k=6) > student-led 0.58 (k=13) > instructor-led 0.51 (k=8).
  • Outcome category significant (Q=8.21, p=.042): creative ability 0.71 > efficiency 0.63 > achievement 0.58 > satisfaction 0.52.
  • Tool type (p=.541), instructional phase (p=.412), design discipline (p=.944) all n.s. — pedagogy configuration, not tool or discipline, is the active ingredient.
  • Prescription: "dialogic mode" (students debate AI outputs under instructor critique); reflective logs recording which AI suggestions were adopted and why others were rejected ("AI sparring"); faculty "AI-Pedagogy Literacy" development. Publication-bias tests mixed (Begg p=.047, Egger p=.359).
DDCT: the L1/L2/L3 evidence backbone for the whole working group — d=0.89 for instructor-student-AI triadic loops vs ~0.5 for unstructured use justifies studio-embedded AI with faculty in the loop (S4, L7); the adopt/reject log is the judgment-notebook spec. Frame it as "pattern evidence, not a causal hierarchy" (authors' own caveat; k=6 in the top subgroup).

2025 (published Nov 2025; filed 2026) — Effects of Customized Generative AI on Student Engagement and Emotions in Visual Communication Design Education

Sustainability 17:9963 (MDPI) · Li, Sun, Kim (Kyungpook National Univ.; experiment in northern China) · randomized quasi-experiment, n=96 juniors (48/48), 6-week "Visual Design and Culture" poster course structured on Double Diamond; experimental = GPT-4 + Stable Diffusion 3.5 with student-trained LoRA
  • Engagement gains (all p<.001): cognitive 3.757→4.229 (Δ0.472), emotional Δ0.396, behavioral Δ0.286; control group flat/n.s. ANCOVA group effects η²p=0.185/0.116/0.148.
  • LoRA recipe is fully specified: 20 images @512px, ~40 min training on one RTX 4060 Ti (16GB), repeat=10, epoch=10, lr=0.0001 — affordable departmental hardware.
  • Emotion interviews (25 students, 347 codes): positive:negative ≈ 2.1:1; top positives excitement (38), satisfaction (32); top negatives stress (26), doubt (22), worry (18). Career confidence was a positive theme (34 codes; "more competitive in the job market").
  • Customization shifts the locus of anxiety: output-unpredictability worry (typical of generic tools) replaced by training-process worry; students report "confident anticipation" and reproducible style instead of "uncertain reception."
  • Guardrails used: sketches/concept boards required before AI generation; every prompt iteration documented with written rationale; tiered prompt teaching + template library.
DDCT: S0 local-model lab + DR11 NDA-safe local models — the 20-image/40-minute LoRA lab on consumer GPUs is the single most concrete deployment spec in this folder (also DR22/DR5 differentiation: Thai-style LoRAs as culturally distinctive assets). Sketch-before-AI gate = Brake·Anchor·Engine; prompt documentation feeds judgment notebooks (L3); DR27 anxiety management via customization. 6-week duration only; engagement, not artwork quality, measured.

2026 — Exploring AI-enhanced design education in China: quasi-experimental study of traditional, chatbot-assisted, and fully integrated AIGC conditions

TechTrends / AECT · Li & Chen (Hunan, China) · quasi-experiment, n=100 VCD undergrads in intact sections of a book design course: Traditional (InDesign, n=33) vs Chatbot-assisted (Kimi/Zhipu/DeepSeek/Doubao at ideation only, n=33) vs Fully integrated AIGC (Kehua platform across all stages, n=34); ANCOVA with pretest + GPA covariates
  • Design product performance: adjusted 85.34 (AIGC) vs 76.37 (chatbot) vs 74.98 (traditional); F(2,95)=18.94, p<.001, η²p=.285; AIGC beats both by +8.96/+10.36 (p<.001); chatbot ≈ traditional (−1.40, p=1.0).
  • Self-efficacy: traditional ≈ AIGC (39.06 vs 38.95, n.s.) but both > chatbot (33.54, p≤.009); the chatbot group's self-efficacy declined slightly (−0.85) while others rose.
  • Role clarity: AIGC 50.38 > chatbot 43.27 (p=.0023); traditional 47.41 intermediate — partial, unstructured AI produces the most role ambiguity.
  • Interviews (n=10): 10/10 report workflow restructuring (poster draft 2 days → half a day; "+80% efficiency"); 10/10 creativity is double-edged (outputs "lack soul", easy to spot); 8/10 prompt competence = the new core skill; 9/10 career anxiety (low-level execution jobs replaced, strategic roles resilient); 10/10 designer becomes "art decision-maker" (film-director analogy).
  • Author caveat: AIGC product scores = performance with AI available, not evidence of independent learning gains.
DDCT: L2 at its sharpest — the middle condition is the warning: sprinkling generic chatbots into an otherwise unchanged course is worse than no AI for self-efficacy/role clarity. For AI-skeptical DDCT students this argues for the 3-zone policy (full workflow integration with explicit human-AI role boundaries, or AI-free), never ad-hoc chatbot use. DR7 identity ("maker → decision-maker" = Direction Ladder), DR27 anxiety, DR15 workflow design.

2026 — Generative AI in creative education: baseline-linked evidence on learning processes, artistic performance, and authorship

Frontiers in Psychology 17:1956939 · Shen, Lee, He (Communication Univ. of Zhejiang + UPM) · baseline-linked observational study, n=133 (of 151) in Character Design + Animation Concept Design; prior grades + 5-dimension blind-scored finals (κ=0.92) + questionnaire; PSM/IPW robustness
  • Process–outcome decoupling: AI users report higher support (PSM ATT +0.365) and skill transfer (+0.486) but no final-score advantage (PSM −1.58, CI [−4.30, 0.95] n.s.); prior course performance is the stable predictor (β=0.46).
  • Authorship penalty: AI users scored lower on the authorship dimension (PSM −1.27, IPW −1.01); odds of a below-max authorship score OR=4.67 (FDR p<.001).
  • Dose response: 0% AI content → authorship 19.38; 1–20% → 19.00; >20% AI-generated content → 17.30 (F=10.34, η²=0.137) — while quality confidence rose (3.36→3.84) and display identity rose (3.26→3.62): students feel more confident as evaluators see less of them.
  • Development outcomes (continuation intention, collaboration, social value) driven by internalization (β=0.40–0.51) and teaching climate, not AI use itself.
  • Prescription: assessment must require process evidence — prompt histories, iteration records, rejected outputs, manual revisions, reflective explanations.
DDCT: DR23/L5/L6 in one study — add "authorship" as a scored rubric dimension and treat ~20% AI-generated content as a disclosure threshold trigger for 🟡-zone artifacts; the >20% cliff gives the 3-zone policy a measurable boundary. Correlational (self-reported AI %), single institution.

2026 — Integrating Generative AI in Higher Art Education: A Systematic Review of Tools, Pedagogies, and Practices

SN Computer Science 7(2):215 (Springer) · Fang (Wuhan Conservatory of Music) · PRISMA systematic review, 27 studies 2023–2025, Scopus+WoS, MMAT-appraised, visual arts/design/music/STEAM
  • Core pattern: the ideation–execution gap — GenAI reliably boosts fluency/divergence at ideation but fails at execution needing nuance/embodied skill; in architecture (Huh et al., n=42) acceptance drops from concept phase to final-presentation phase ("U-shaped utility").
  • Creativity evidence is discipline-dependent: music strongest (Wang: 566 students, significant TTCT gains with ChatGPT); Kollia et al. found fluency/flexibility gains but no originality gain; de-skilling warning (Tsidylo & Sena, n=39: "lack of fundamental knowledge" among DALL-E 2/Midjourney users).
  • Deployment mode is a pedagogical choice: web interfaces → access/ideation; API/local (Stable Diffusion, IBM Watson Beat) → deeper critical engagement, executional control, ethics — but demand infrastructure and instructor expertise.
  • Institutional readiness divide: specialized art colleges more resistant than comprehensive universities (2 surveys, 108 educators); homogenization risk (Doshi & Hauser); ethics (authorship/IP, Western-canon bias, black-box) should be embedded in studio critique, not bolt-on modules.
DDCT: DR9 honest pipeline placement + DR13 durable-vs-churn + L4 — supports placing AI in ideation while protecting execution fundamentals (L6), and justifies S0's local-model lab (deployment depth = pedagogical depth); the art-college-resistance finding speaks directly to DDCT's AI-skeptic culture (DR7).

2026 — The Impact of Generative Artificial Intelligence on Student Creativity in Art and Design Education: A Meta-Analysis

Journal of Curriculum Studies Research 8(2):340-363 · Özkan (Kütahya Dumlupınar Univ., Türkiye) · PRISMA meta-analysis, 31 studies (2023–2026; 61.3% undergraduate samples; 54.8% vertical-collectivist contexts), random-effects, CMA v4, coder κ=.91
  • Overall effect on creativity d=0.61 (95% CI [0.49, 0.73]), Q=284.63 p<.01; trim-and-fill trimmed 0 studies (d unchanged) — converges with Chiang's d=0.60 on a different study set.
  • Academic level (Qb=19.37, p<.05): graduate 0.74 > undergraduate 0.63 > K-12 0.48 — domain knowledge amplifies AI's creative value.
  • Creativity dimension (Qb=21.04, p<.05): originality/innovation 0.69 > general creativity 0.60 > creative self-efficacy 0.58 > divergent thinking 0.54 — AI helps idea novelty more than divergent-thinking capacity.
  • Tool type (Qb=8.93, p<.01): text-to-image 0.66 > LLM 0.60 > GAN/deep-learning 0.52 > mixed 0.43; publication year 2024 peak 0.66 (2023: 0.46; 2025-26: 0.60); culture n.s. (collectivist 0.65 vs individualist 0.55).
  • Guidance: level-sensitive integration, T2I-first tool selection for visual disciplines, AI at ideation paired with traditional pedagogy for self-efficacy/divergent thinking.
DDCT: L6 quantified — creativity gains are positive but uneven (divergent thinking and self-efficacy gain least → protect these with non-AI pedagogy); level moderation (grad > UG > K-12) supports S1's AI-off years 1–2 with heavier AI at Y3+; T2I-first finding informs S0 tool-sprint tool choice. Caveats: heterogeneous Q, self-report measures, many vertical-collectivist samples (Thai context adjacent).

Top 3 cases for the DDCT curriculum

1. The DDDOS per-stage AI-gating sheet (Song & Xu, 2026)

What was done: In a 4-week character-design course (n=120, 3 arms), the winning condition gave students a Double-Diamond Design Operation Sheet specifying stage objectives, self-monitoring questions, and which AI tool was permitted at each stage (text-LLM for Discover/Define; image-gen for Develop/Deliver; image-gen deliberately banned during Define to prevent premature visual fixation), plus stage-completion checks — no extra instructor time. · Measured result: design achievement 20.90 vs 18.65 (GAI-only) vs 16.13 (traditional), F=43.28, p<.001; creative self-efficacy 4.25 vs 3.84 vs 3.54; problem-solving 4.44 vs 4.04 vs 3.71 (all p<.001). · Strengthens: S0 (AI-Fluency core + 3-week sprints), DR15 workflow design, DR23 assessment integrity, L1/L3 — adopt the sheet as the standard scaffold inside every DDCT studio assignment; it operationalizes the 3-zone policy (🟢/🟡/🔴 by stage) inside a single brief.

2. The 3-integration-mode quasi-experiment: no ad-hoc chatbots; full workflow or none (Li & Chen, 2026)

What was done: n=100 book-design students in three authentic conditions — traditional, general-purpose chatbots at ideation only (Kimi/Zhipu/DeepSeek/Doubao), and a fully integrated AIGC platform (Kehua) embedded across ideation→generation→revision with teacher guidance on using outputs critically. · Measured result: full integration beat both others on product score (adj. 85.34 vs 76.37 vs 74.98, η²p=.285) and role clarity (50.38 vs 43.27, p=.0023), but the chatbot arm was no better than traditional on products and significantly worse on self-efficacy (33.54 vs 39.06/38.95) — partial, unstructured AI actively erodes confidence and role clarity. · Strengthens: L2, S1/S0 governance, DR27 (anxiety), DR7 — for KMUTT's AI-skeptical cohort this is the strongest argument that AI must enter either as a fully scaffolded, role-explicit workflow (with explicit human-AI responsibility boundaries) or stay in the 🔴 zone; ban "just let them play with ChatGPT" as a policy option.

3. Student-trained LoRA customization lab (Li, Sun & Kim, 2025)

What was done: 96 VCD juniors, 6-week Double-Diamond poster course; each student trained a personal LoRA on Stable Diffusion 3.5 (20 images @512px, ~40 min per model, repeat=10/epoch=10/lr=0.0001 on a single RTX 4060 Ti 16GB) with GPT-4 for concept work; guardrails: concept board required before any generation, every prompt iteration logged with written rationale, tiered prompt training. · Measured result: engagement rose on all three dimensions (cognitive Δ+0.472, emotional +0.396, behavioral +0.286, all p<.001; ANCOVA η²p up to 0.185) while controls stayed flat; emotions ran 2.1:1 positive; customization shifted anxiety from "will the output be usable" to "can I configure the system" and produced career-confidence gains (34 codes). · Strengthens: S0 local-model lab, DR11 NDA-safe local models, DR22/DR5 Thai differentiation (train LoRAs on Thai visual culture as sellable, owned assets), L3 — a ready bill-of-materials for DDCT: one consumer GPU per 4–5 students can run the whole lab offline.

  • Two meta-analyses converge (Chiang d=0.60, N=2,847; Özkan d=0.61, 31 studies) but both flag heterogeneity/short interventions; Chiang's top usage-mode subgroup has only k=6 studies and mixed publication-bias tests — quote as patterns, not causal rankings.
  • Publication-culture skew: 11 of 16 readable papers are greater-China studies, mostly positive-toned; AI-skeptic student voices appear only inside mixed findings (papers 2, 5, 12). No Thailand-specific or Southeast-Asia evidence in this folder — DR17–22 (Thai industry) must be argued from local data plus these generic mechanisms.
  • Internal inconsistencies to caveat: paper 4 (animation GAI) reports n=120 but table subgroups n=80/40 and df's of 98/58, plus 8/10/12-week arms — treat its large effect sizes cautiously. Paper 7 reports TK→CK as β=0.26 and β=0.37 in different sections. Paper 9's ANCOVA F values (803, 461) with SDs of 0.1–0.2 are statistically possible but extreme.
  • What nobody measured: long-term skill retention beyond ~12 weeks (only one study had a delayed follow-up interview round); portfolio labor-market value; effect on foundational craft skill development (only de-skiling warnings, no measured atrophy); faculty-development interventions (readiness diagnosed in papers 7/15/16, but no training-program evaluation).
  • Assessment consensus without a common instrument: papers 3, 6, 13, 16 all converge on process evidence (prompt logs, decision trails, rejected outputs, AI-use statements) but each uses a different rubric — DDCT could contribute by standardizing one authorship-aware rubric across its studios.
  • Authorship dose-threshold (paper 13) is a single-site observational result (self-reported AI %) — pilot the >20% disclosure trigger before hard-coding it in policy.
