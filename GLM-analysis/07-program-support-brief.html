2 · Supporting materials & equipment

⭐ The single most imitable move: a personal workstation for every student, for the whole program

CMU ETC: "It's not shared, clustered, or lab-based. For the two years you're here, it's yours and yours alone." At ~60 students this is a bounded, hugely marketable budget line — and it directly serves the LoRA/local-model strategy (a personal GPU box doubles as the local-model lab node).

ETC Facilities & Technology
  • Equipment lending library — cameras, VR/AR headsets, audio gear, tablets, peripherals, checked out like books (NYU Game Center Tech Resources).
  • Game & media library — NYU's Open Library holds 3,000+ games from Atari to current consoles and doubles as club/tournament space. DDCT version: playable game history + a Thai cultural asset archive (scans, textures, motifs — the RISD Nature Lab idea, localized; feeds DR22 Thai-LoRA identity).
  • Console/hardware devkits — apply to PlayStation First (Sony's academic licensing program: devkits + support; Abertay built Europe's largest PS teaching lab on it) and equivalent Nintendo/Microsoft academic programs.
  • License & credits bundle — GitHub Education Pack, JetBrains, Unity/Unreal academic, Adobe, Autodesk + AWS/Azure/GCP credits; NVIDIA DLI certifications for CT students.
  • Specialist rooms — mocap studio (Tokyo Polytechnic), CAVE/VR room + playtest lab with a coordinator (ETC runs a university-wide playtesting hub — also a research asset).

3 · Organizations students can belong to (and run)

  • A student-game publishing label — USC Games Publishing is the world's first academic label putting student games on Steam/consoles. A "DDCT Press" that publishes the best student work is marketing, portfolio proof, and revenue loop in one.
  • Summer Bridge-style incubator — 10 weeks, industry mentors, for the most promising capstone teams (USC model).
  • Chapters & jams — IGDA + ACM SIGGRAPH student chapters, Women in Games; become an official Global Game Jam host site (L8 evidence: jam formats are validated AI-learning media).
  • Annual public Expo + Industry Day — USC's Expo draws hundreds of industry guests; PUCPR's Game Show makes every student publish each semester. DDCT version doubles as the recruiting event the liaison officer runs.
  • Alumni-mentor network — structured from year 1, fed by the two-advisor system.

4 · Money converted into experiences

  • Festival & competition fund — cover entry fees + travel: IGF student category, SIGGRAPH, Student Academy Awards, Annecy, Thailand Game Show / BIDC, and industry-run pipelines like GradsInGames' Search for a Star (recruiter-facing). Abertay's Dare Academy shows a university can run its own funded competition.
  • Per-project production budget — each studio team gets a small real budget (assets, printing, actors, licenses) and must account for it: production literacy by default (DR14–15).
  • IP & business clinic — access to IP legal advice (DR10 IP fog is a top industry concern) + freelancer/business-of-creativity workshops (DR1–8 career defense).
  • Conference community ritual — USC gathers its community at GDC yearly; DDCT's equivalent: a yearly presence at Thailand Game Show for current students + alumni.
