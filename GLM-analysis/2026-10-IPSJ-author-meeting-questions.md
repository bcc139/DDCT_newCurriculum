# Questions for the IPSJ author meeting (Kondo) — prepared 2026-10-14

Paper: Kondo & Sato, *LLM-Assisted Algorithmic Art Education for Humanities Students: A Computational
Thinking-Based Evaluation*, JIP Vol.26 (accepted 2026-07-02). Local copy: `../2026-10-IPSJ-paper.pdf`.
Context: first-year law students (N=100, 80 valid), NL prompts (Japanese) → AI-generated p5.js → CG art,
one 90-min session, 8-element CT framework (C1–C8), self-report only.

## Priority order

1. **Instrument access** — will you share the full 17-item questionnaire (JP + EN) and the open-ended
   coding scheme? Any inter-rater validation planned for the C1–C8 framework?
2. **The obvious next study** — the paper notes prompt logs were NOT collected. What objective metrics
   would you extract from logs (revision count, constraint specificity, decomposition granularity)?
   Would you co-design a log-collecting version? **DDCT could be the pilot site** (design students =
   the missing population).
3. **Population transfer** — law students ≠ art/design students. Expect visually-trained students to
   score differently (higher contextual reasoning / pattern recognition)? Any replication with design
   cohorts planned?
4. **The 75% motivation claim** — "want to continue learning programming": did anyone actually continue?
   Any follow-up on sustained motivation beyond the single session?
5. **Decomposition granularity** — students struggled with "how detailed should the breakdown be?"
   What scaffolds help; where is the sweet spot?
6. **Contextual reasoning scored lowest** — the paper proposes presentations/peer review as the fix;
   has that been tested?
7. **Language/culture** — prompts were Japanese. Observations on non-English prompting quality?
   (Relevant: Thai students prompting in Thai vs English.)
8. **Scope** — the paper cites vibe coding. Does C1–C8 survive scaling from a single artwork to a full
   production workflow (assets, scenes, pipelines)?

## Tactical note
Offering a concrete collaboration (Q2: a co-designed prompt-log study at DDCT using his rubric) is
likely to land better than a long question list. Q1–Q3 are the natural conversation openers.
