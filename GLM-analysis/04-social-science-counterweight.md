**For:** DDCT curriculum working group

## PART I — The principle

### 1. The principle, sharpened

What RPI actually did is more radical than "adding social science." It made the degree *about the questions AI cannot answer*. RPI defines game studies as a field that "synthesizes literary, social scientific, artistic, and computational approaches to understand how games, computational media, and play shape society, technological development, culture, and the human sense of self" — and then puts analysis seminars **beside** the design studios, not behind them. The making and the meaning are co-equal.

Why this counters "too much AI" structurally rather than rhetorically:

- **AI saturates the *how*; social science owns the *why and for-whom*.** A curriculum that is all AI tools teaches students to answer questions nobody asked. The studies spine keeps question-asking alive as a graded, defendable skill.
- **It reframes AI from solution to object.** XJTLU's *AI and Big Data in Society*, USC's *Center for Generative AI and Society*, and FABW's ethics-first AI Staff Unit all institutionalize the same move: the school studies AI as much as it trains on it.
- **It is the honest version of "ethics."** Ethics pasted onto a tool course is decoration. A graded seminar where students defend positions on labor, IP, and cultural impact — RPI requires an "evidence-based paper supporting or challenging media claims about AI" — is ethics with teeth.

### 2. Three functions: Brake · Anchor · Engine

| Function | What it does | What it looks like in courses | Archive anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🛑 **Brake** | Names what AI *shouldn't* do | ethics/IP/labor seminars; disclosure & governance practice; "kill criteria" case studies | FABW AI Staff Unit + ethics framework · DigiPen CS394/594 ethics module · the 120k-jobs study debate (Reuters) |
| ⚓ **Anchor** | Holds what AI *can't* do | player & culture studies; meaning/form analysis; "who is this for and is it worth making?" | Tampere's four perspectives · RPI seminars · Grace's critical gameplay |
| ⚙️ **Engine** | Makes AI *useful* | player research methods; market/culture analysis; computational social science on real data | XJTLU's NLP/sentiment/agent-based-modeling research cluster · games-user-research teaching (Uppsala, Aalto) · TAW's game-production-studies theme |

The counterweight is strongest when all three run — a purely critical program produces cynics; a purely technical program produces prompt-operatives.

## PART II — The evidence: bold course-level moves worldwide

Individual courses (not whole programs) that make the humanities/social-science move, verified in this project's archive (2026-10).

### 3. AI-and-society courses (the newest boldness)

| Course | School | What's bold |
|---|---|---|
| **Artificial Intelligence and Big Data in Society** (COM333) | XJTLU (comms school) | AI studied as a societal force inside a *communication* degree, not CS |

### 7. The pattern — and the gap that matters to DDCT

Every bold course does one of three things: **(a)** studies AI instead of only training it; **(b)** treats theory/method as a *named, graded, defended* co-equal with making; or **(c)** brings an identity/cultural lens into a tech or art faculty — usually by **embedding the scholar in the tech unit** (Ruberg in Informatics; LaPensée dual-appointed; Uppsala's department parked in the Faculty of Arts). Organization precedes culture.

**Verified gap:** no *Thai/Southeast Asian cultural content* or *Thai games industry studies* course exists anywhere in the catalog — the regional slot XJTLU occupies for China is empty for Thailand. That is DDCT's most defensible bold-course opening: *Gaming Innovations in Thailand* (the COM419 homologue), *Thai Games & Soft Power*, *Isan/Buddhist folklore in interactive media*, plus the sociology-of-media-professionals model pointed at TACGA's own industry.

## PART III — The DDCT design

### 8. A concrete social-science spine for DDCT (blocks/seminars, not a department)

1. **Critical Game Design Seminar** (every year, RPI-style rotating topics: cultural heritage & historical simulation · phenomenology of play · participatory play research · *Thai games and soft power*).
2. **Players & Cultures** (Tampere perspectives 1–2): player research methods, fandom studies, audience analysis — *employable*: games-user-research is a real studio role.
3. **Production Studies**: how studios, pipelines, and jobs change under AI (28% layoff data, outsourcing shifts, union responses).
4. **AI & Society** (the XJTLU COM333 model): FABW-style governance practice — disclosure, compliance, local-model ethics.
5. **Thai & Regional Cultural Content Studies**: the CAFA digital-heritage model shows cultural scholarship and AI co-authoring — DDCT's distinctive version of the counterweight.
6. **Methods block**: qualitative (interviews, observation) + computational (NLP, sentiment, network analysis on games data) — social science *with* data.

### 9. Why this converts AI-hostile students (DR8)

The spine gives skeptics a **rigorous scholarly path** instead of a dead end: they analyze, critique, and govern AI (⚓/🛑) while peers run the production floor (⚙️). RPI literally graduates critics-who-make; craft-first Atelier missions plus this spine do the same. **Resistance becomes research** — the strongest de-escalation available, at zero identity cost.

### 10. Why it serves the direction-first thesis (`03`), not just opposes AI

Every direction skill is social-science-fed: taste needs audience understanding; critique needs theory's vocabulary; product judgment needs production studies' map; business judgment needs cultural/market reading. The counterweight is the direction curriculum's *fuel line*. (AI handles the how; the spine supplies the what-for.)

### 11. Implementation notes

- **Staffing:** KMUTT is a technology university — build a cross-faculty alliance with liberal-arts/humanities units (joint appointments, team-taught blocks) + practitioner-adjuncts from TACGA. The Uppsala/Tampere/Ruberg pattern: embed the scholar where the makers are.
- **Assessment that survives AI naturally:** in-class essays, oral defenses (vivas), published critiques, seminar debate — the cheapest integrity zone you own (DR23).
- **Plugs into every scenario:** Scenario 0's ethics thread + fluency course · Scenario 1 Track A's theory spine · Scenario 3's research-exit route · Scenario 4's "studies missions" · Scenario 5 L1's ethics content.
- **Governance formula:** a visible **balance rule** — *every AI-production credit earns a judgment/studies credit* (≈1:1 in years 3–4) — owned by the AI Curriculum Council with student seats. Ratios are debatable; having one converts "counter AI" from sentiment into policy.
- **Two failure modes:** (a) *ethics-washing* — grade by defended argument, not attendance; (b) *counterweight-as-silence* — USC's caution shows critique-only leaves graduates unequipped; keep ⚙️ running beside 🛑 and ⚓.
