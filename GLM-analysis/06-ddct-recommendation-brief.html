0 · The constraints define the answer

DDCT is not a cohort university — it is a boutique atelier with a joint-venture brand. Fewer than 10 CT students and ~50 DD students per year, international delivery, DigiPen partnership, premium tuition. At this scale, the DigiPen-style catalog (dozens of specialized lockstep courses, 240 credits) is unsustainable for CT and unnecessary for DD. Everything below follows from taking the small scale seriously as a feature (1:1 attention, shipped work, industry access) rather than fighting it with a big-school curriculum.

Precedents: CMU ETC (tiny elite studio, dedicated staff) · ETC curriculum model · Animationsinstitut (studio-apprenticeship model) · EKA Estonia (small art academy, research-creation) · Salford × BBC/ITV (industry co-location) · Hxsd×Tripo (vendor embeds in a small school)

1 · Diagnosis

  • Evidence says: AI helps when scaffolded and hurts when raw (chatbot-arm worst self-efficacy, η²p=.285; skill-formation −17%); effects are modest after bias correction (g≈0.32); creativity/divergent thinking is the weakest gain (d=0.54) — protect fundamentals, gate the AI, grade the judgment. Full evidence map: 00-INDEX.html.
  • World scan says: the field is splitting into "game/dev-first" (DigiPen, GaTech CM, U-tad) and "experience-first" (NYU IMA, RCA IED, Aalto New Media, Silpakorn aiDEX) programs. Nobody owns the junction — a Digital-Experience degree with real production engineering depth. Full scan: curriculum/README.md.
  • Industry scan says: six partnership models exist (curriculum embedding · ambassador/access · spinout · co-location · company-run institutes · industry-owned credentials). DDCT currently uses almost none. Full taxonomy: industry-partnerships/README.md.
  • Structural reality says: CT at <10/yr cannot field teams, electives, or specializations alone. DD at ~50/yr can. The fix is not marketing — it is merging the teaching apparatus while keeping distinct credentials. Precedents: U-tad's three-degree convergence, Tokyo Polytechnic's plan/design/program split, HSE's Design×CS joint program.

2 · Recommended architecture — one program, two passports

Keep DD and CT as named degrees (DigiPen channel + accreditation), but run them as one vertical studio system where every production team is mixed DD+CT and CT seniors act as technical directors. Modeled on U-tad's three-degree convergence, Tokyo Polytechnic's 企画/デザイン/プログラム split, and HSE's Design×CS joint program.

Year 1 — Foundations (AI-off 🔴)Drawing, design systems, math/programming bootcamps, film/media grammar. The one exception: AI-Fluency Core (Lehigh blueprint, production-pipeline spine) — with tools withheld until mid-course and AI framed as a situational tool, never a mandated evil (UCF "Art of AI"); students co-author the course AI policy (Hamline model). Brain-first sequencing everywhere.
Year 2 — Gated studios (🟡)3-week sprint blocks; every brief carries a DDDOS per-stage AI-gating sheet; 3-column AI-use protocol + judgment notebook from day 1 (operationalized with workflow mapping — anticipated vs actual — and SFSU's 10 validation methods); mixed DD+CT teams ship publicly each semester.
Year 3 — The fork (🟢/🟡)Routes not schools: Craft & Direction vs AI-Augmented Production (Parsons-pathway style, informally routed inside one studio system). LoRA customization lab; NDA-safe local models; real client briefs.
Year 4 — Capstone & passportMixed capstone with industry mentor; four-pillar assessment (product ≤20%); viva for every student (trivial at this size); public Expo; portfolio = shipped works + judgment notebooks + process logs.
🟢 Required AIAI-Fluency Core · LoRA lab · AI-augmented production route
🟡 Disclosed AIStudio briefs with DDDOS gating · 3-column protocol · prompt logs graded
🔴 AI-freeY1 foundations · in-lab extensions · oral defenses · unaided bookends before every AI phase

Evidence anchors: Maribor package (null harm with anchors, p≥0.73) · Klapproth unaided bookends (retention 57.5→68.5%) · duration sweet spot 4–16 wks (folder-1 digest) · foundations-buy-quality (grad +1.01 vs UG +0.43)

4 · The industry stack (six models → concrete first partners)

  • This semester, free: apply to Meshy education plan (Thai case exists), Tripo ambassador, Runway educators (NYU precedent), NVIDIA DLI GenAI teaching kit (free slides/labs/certificates).
  • This year, cheap: Netflix×Geidai-style masterclasses with Thai studios; D&AD New Blood briefs inside existing courses (zero cost, industry-curated assessment).

  • Rethink the DigiPen deal along the CUC axis: China's CUC runs DigiPen content with its own teaching staff — KMUTT could shift toward "DigiPen blueprint + KMUTT delivery + dual credential", which also solves small-cohort scheduling.

5 · Out-of-the-box ideas worth exploring

A · "Every CT student is a studio." With <10 CT students/year, invert the cohort: each Y3–Y4 CT student runs a production cell — pitches a project, staffs it with DD students, delivers to a real SME client, owns the tech-direction. CT becomes an apprenticeship in directing production, not sitting courses. (Direction Ladder made literal; DR19–20.)

Precedent flavor: ETC project-course model · DADIU cross-institution productions

B · Sell the outcome, not the seat. Premium tuition buys a published contract: "8 shipped public works, a verified-process portfolio (judgment notebooks), an industry-mentored capstone, and a viva-defended degree." Marketing the 3-zone map openly also converts AI-skeptical families (the 🔴 zones are the value protection story) — framed as situational choice, never a mandated evil ("anti-broccoli framing"; students who choose not to use AI are celebrated as reaching "creative sovereignty").

Assessment basis: four-pillar + viva kit · Maribor (permissive-AI-safe evidence) · UCF Art of AI framing

C · DADIU-consortium teaching. Share studio blocks with Silpakorn ICT, CAMT CMU, Walailak — mixed inter-university teams (fills CT cohort gaps, builds the national network, costs little).

Precedents: DADIU (consortium game program) · Silpakorn aiDEX · CAMT CMU · Walailak IMAG · demand proof: Thai Meshy classroom

D · Night atelier / TAICA stacking. Open Y2–Y3 studio blocks to working professionals (stackable micro-credentials, S5): fills seats at marginal cost, creates mentor pool, feeds the Attach-and-Train S3 revenue line. Scenario definitions: 02-curriculum-scenarios.md.

E · The spinout direction. ShanghaiTech→Deemos shows a university can generate the 3D-AI vendor. Position the CT lab as a creative-AI R&D cell (tools, LoRAs, pipelines) with tech-transfer upside — a different identity than "teaches Unity".

Case: Deemos/Rodin ShanghaiTech incubation · the 40-spinout ecosystem

F · Two advisors per student. One faculty + one industry mentor, feasible only at this size; makes the boutique claim real and doubles the placement network. Detail: 07-program-support-brief.html §1.

G · Prompt-to-code art as the DD↔CT bridge + a CT rubric for the notebook. Kondo & Sato's IPSJ study had first-year *law* students (zero programming) write natural-language prompts → AI-generated p5.js code → artworks: "visualization of thought." 87–90% self-reported decomposition/abstraction/algorithmic-thinking gains, 84% "learned to collaborate with AI," α=0.87 — and their 8-element CT framework (4 core: decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithmic thinking + 4 extended: debugging, recomposition, contextual reasoning, visualization/prediction) is a ready-made rubric for grading DDCT's prompt-iteration notebooks, especially on the DD side. Use the exercise as the first shared DD+CT studio (DD meets algorithmic thinking; CT meets aesthetic judgment). Cite as an instrument, not an effects benchmark — single 90-min session, self-report, no control, no prompt logs.

Kondo & Sato 2026, JIP Vol.26 (PDF, 17 pp.) · author meeting planned — question list

6 · Program supports beyond classes (the boutique layer)

Small cohorts + a larger budget are exactly the conditions under which elite programs (CMU ETC, USC Games, NYU Game Center) out-spend on experience, not credits. Full research in 07-program-support-brief.html; the essentials:

PeopleDedicated studio technical director + career & industry liaison + producer-in-residence; d.school-style coaching pool; two advisors per student (faculty + industry mentor). ETC staff model
ThingsPersonal workstation for every student, whole program (ETC policy — flagship promise; doubles as LoRA/local-model lab) · equipment lending library (NYU model) · game library + Thai cultural asset archive (NYU Open Library) · PlayStation First devkits · license/credit bundle.
Organizations"DDCT Press" student-game publishing label (USC model) · Summer-Bridge incubator (USC model) · IGDA/SIGGRAPH chapters + Global Game Jam site · annual Expo & Industry Day · alumni-mentor network.
Money-as-experienceFestival & competition fund (IGF, SIGGRAPH, Annecy, Thailand Game Show, Search for a Star) · per-project production budgets · IP & business clinic (answers DR10) · yearly community ritual at a flagship event.

Sequence it: semester-1 = near-zero-cost items (advisor pairs, licenses, program applications, jam site) · year-1 = the two hires + workstation policy · year-2 = publishing label + incubator. Don't promise supports before they're staffed — under-resourced promises become marketing liabilities.

7 · Honest risks

  • DigiPen/ABET-style accreditation locks may resist merged-studio delivery — the CUC×DigiPen structure is the negotiation precedent.
  • Two formal tracks at <10 students fragments teaching — routes must stay informal (Parsons pathways) until volume justifies more.
  • Faculty load is the real constraint (L7) — moves 1–6 all cost staff time; sequence them, don't launch all at once. (ChemE rebuild: assessments cost more than syllabi)
  • CT enrollment is a funnel problem, not only a curriculum problem — the public-ship contract, co-branded industry modules, and the support layer (§6: workstation, advisors, publishing label) are the demand-side answers.
  • Support promises outrunning staffing — a publishing label or festival fund without a liaison officer and technical director becomes a liability; phase supports behind the two hires.
  • The MIT cognitive-debt and offloading evidence is correlational/preprint in parts (MIT EEG; Gerlich) — adopt brain-first sequencing as design principle, cite carefully.
