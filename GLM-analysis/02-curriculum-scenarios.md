# Curriculum Scenarios for DDCT (KMUTT) — Generative-AI Era
**Companion files:** `01-concerns-breakdown.md` (DR codes cited below) · `kmutt-visual-guide.html`
**Design stance:** none of these is "the answer" — they are six deployable configurations, mixable in phases. A recommended portfolio appears in §8.

---

## 0. Design principles (derived from the concerns file)

1. **Judgment over execution** — every course must grow evaluation, direction, and taste, not just output (DR5, DR6, synthesis).
2. **Taste before tools** — craft foundations precede AI-tool access; AI is *unlocked by demonstrated fundamentals*, not granted by semester number (DR3).
3. **Mechanisms over menu** — teach how generative models work and how to evaluate/absorb the *next* tool (DigiPen CS 394/594 pattern), never a single-vendor tool menu (DR7, DR14).
4. **Parallel track, honest map** — teach AI where it actually sits today (pre-production ideation, structured execution assist, lookdev), not as a magic replacement layer (DR9).
5. **Apprenticeship rebuilt in-house** — the program itself must simulate the junior→senior ladder the industry stopped funding (DR1, DR4).
6. **Slow/fast layering** — degree architecture changes slowly; tool modules refresh yearly as short blocks (DR26).
7. **Agency, not mandate** — legitimate AI-free space + student co-governance of the AI policy (DR8); name degrees around *direction & production*, not "AI" (DR22).

## Scenario 0 — INFUSE: update DD & CT in place *(12 months, lowest risk)*

Keep both current programs; retrofit an AI spine.

| Layer | Change |
|---|---|
| **New required course (Y1, both programs)** | *AI Fluency for Designers & Technologists* — Ringling's four principles (delegation, description, discernment, diligence) mapped onto **UNESCO's student AI-competency framework**; covers what genAI is/isn't, where it sits in pipelines (DR9), ethics/IP basics, disclosure practice |
| **Ethics & law thread** | Thai copyright + client-market compliance (EU AI Act awareness) woven into existing humanities/professional-practice courses (DR11, DR19) |
| **Studio augmentation** | Y2 studios: optional AI-augmented assignment variants; Y4 capstone: dual path — *craft-built* OR *AI-augmented* (the latter requires a making-of dossier + disclosure form) (DR2, DR16) |
| **Tool-agnostic sprint blocks** | Two 3-week block electives per year ("Tool Sprint: image/video gen", "Tool Sprint: agent pipelines") — content replaced yearly (DR7, DR26) |
| **Local-model lab** | Open-weight models (image/video/LLM) on department GPUs → NDA-safe, cheap, sovereign (DR12, DR25; FABW precedent) |
| **Policy** | 3-zone AI policy (see §6) + admissions-portfolio language like DigiPen/Sheridan (DR23) |

*Pros:* fast, no approvals beyond course-level, builds faculty muscle. *Cons:* doesn't differentiate DDCT; AI remains a garnish; resistant students stay resistant.
*Precedents:* Ringling AI Fluency/certificate; Sheridan's explorations; Futuregames' policy-first approach.

## Scenario 1 — TWO-TRACK UNDERGRAD: split *inside* the program, at Year 3 *(18–24 months)*

Your fine-arts/digital-arts analogy — but with the crucial lesson from the research: **separate incoming students into whole programs and you calcify identities and create hierarchy** (the SCAD controversy; the "AI degree" stigma). DigiPen itself did **not** split its BFA internally — it kept art foundations AI-light and built AI depth in a *separate engineering degree*. The workable version:

- **Y1–2 shared, AI-off craft/tech core** (drawing, design fundamentals, 3D foundations, math/programming for CT) — identical for all; the AI-free zone is *honored* here (DR3, DR8).
- **Y3 fork into named concentrations** (not "AI vs anti-AI" — frame by destination):
  - **Track A — Craft & Direction:** mastery of the traditional pipeline + critique + art direction; AI studied as *object* (critical use, ethics, evaluation) — RPI-style critical lens.
  - **Track B — AI-Augmented Production:** control techniques (ControlNet-class, LoRA fine-tuning), pipeline integration (USD, engines, ComfyUI graphs), hallucination-reduction/evaluation (DR10, DR13, DR14) — DigiPen-CS394/594-style mechanisms.
- **Y4 mixed capstone teams** — a shipped game/film where Track A students direct and Track B students run AI pipelines (mirrors DigiPen's cross-disciplinary team model, where AI specialists join art/design teams).

*Pros:* real depth without ghettoizing anyone; both tracks need each other in capstone → mutual respect; matches industry's direction/production split (DR5, DR6).
*Cons:* scheduling complexity; needs enough Y3 students per track; faculty must staff two tracks.
*Precedents:* DigiPen's program-level split + team projects; SCAD minor-inside-majors; AAU's multi-degree sprawl (what to avoid).

## Scenario 2 — NEW UNDERGRAD DEGREE: B.Sc. **Creative Technology & AI** *(24–36 months incl. approvals)*

A four-year sibling to CT (or absorbed into CT as a named concentration if approvals are slow), using the **DigiPen BS-in-AI blueprint** you have direct access to (DR28):

- **Spine:** math (linear algebra, probability, data science) → programming (Python, C++/C#) → ML/deep learning (PyTorch) → generative media (image/video/3D/motion, LLM agents, RAG) → **engine embedding (Unity/Unreal)** → evaluation methods → ethics/IP.
- **Every year:** team production project (games/films/tools) with DD students as clients/teammates — AI students ship real things, not notebooks.
- **Distinctive DDCT twist (DR22):** "Thai content, global pipelines" — capstones around Thai cultural IP with compliance-ready production (DR17, DR19).

*Pros:* strongest market signal; feeds grad school; DigiPen can accelerate design. *Cons:* heaviest approvals & hiring; risks being "a CS degree in disguise"; small first cohorts.
*Precedents:* DigiPen BS CS in AI; SCAD Applied AI BDes (and its controversy — involve industry + students early to avoid the backlash); NTU MSc w/ GenAI focus (grad-level analogue).

## Scenario 3 — GRADUATE PROGRAM: M.Sc./M.F.A. **Creative AI (Design & Production)** *(18–30 months)*

Thailand's contraction (DR17, DR18) makes a graduate/retraining layer the *highest-urgency* piece — the industry's current workforce needs reskilling now, and faculty/research capacity grows here.

- **Format:** 18–24 months, weekend/online-hybrid for professionals + full-time research route.
- **Two exit paths:** (a) *Practice route* — industry brief + portfolio; (b) *Research route* — thesis **with optional creative component** (NTU ADM's "Thesis and Creative Component" architecture) → seeds a future PhD.
- **Attach-and-Train variant (DigiPen Singapore's AnT template):** a 4+2-month certificate (4 classroom + 2 embedded with partner studios), co-funded with **depa/TACGA**, converting mid-career artists into AI-augmented leads — directly answers the Thai workforce contraction.
- *Pros:* fastest differentiation; industry-funded; builds research; no undergrad-resistance problem (adults opt in). *Cons:* needs studio partners + evening faculty; revenue per head lower.
- *Precedents:* DigiPen Singapore AnT → Specialist Diploma; NFTS 6-month AI course; Bournemouth MSc AI for Media; FABW's professional-partnership model.

## Scenario 4 — UNORTHODOX: the **Mission-Driven Atelier** *(pilot in 12 months with a volunteer cohort of 20–30)*

Your instinct about advisor-matched, student-customized, packed-course structures maps onto proven models — combined here into one design:

**The four imported mechanisms**

1. **Individualized pathway + matched adviser (NYU Gallatin model):** each student writes a **Learning Agreement** — a mission ("AI-assisted Thai folklore animation shorts", "procedural environments for indie games", "critical/anti-AI craft practice") — and the program *matches a primary faculty adviser* (+ an industry mentor). Concentrations are student-authored, rigor-checked by committee.
2. **Passion/problem-based studio platform (Aalto Design Factory model):** a physical atelier where projects, not courses, are the unit; industry co-creation; bootcamp culture.
3. **Block intensives (Colorado College Block Plan / block-mode literature):** one subject at a time in 3-week immersion blocks — *Drawing Bootcamp → ComfyUI Deep-Dive → Character Pipeline → Game-Jam Production → Research Sprint* — with spaced re-touch sessions to counter the retention tail-off the literature warns about (DR26).
4. **Vertical studios + competency badges:** every term, Y1–Y4 students mix in production teams with rotating roles (junior → lead → director); blocks and roles stack into *badge-verified competencies* (craft, control, direction, research, compliance) that jointly constitute the degree.

**Why this is the strongest answer to your students' AI hostility:** opting into a mission (including explicitly craft-first missions) converts resistance into authorship; the AI-off craft missions are as legitimate as the AI-heavy ones — same degree architecture (DR8, DR22).

*Pros:* the most future-proof and the best junior→senior machine; attracts the students weekly lectures lose. *Cons:* advising load is real; QA of individualized paths needs a strong committee; registrar friction.

## Scenario 5 — ALLIANCE HUB: **Thai Creative-AI Academy** (TAICA-style micro-credentials) *(9–12 months)*

KMUTT/DDCT hosts a stackable, open-to-all credential ladder — Taiwan's TAICA is the proof this works at national scale:

- **Level 1 — AI Fluency (3 weeks):** open to *all* KMUTT students + industry; Scenario 0's course in block form.
- **Level 2 — Applied blocks (3 weeks each):** image/video generation for production; 3D/gen pipelines; agent workflows; ethics & compliance — cross-enrollable with partner universities (TAICA model) and studios.
- **Level 3 — Studio intensive (8–12 weeks):** sponsored briefs with TACGA/depa studios (the CDM × Inworld AI pattern); stackable into Scenarios 2–4 for credit.

*Pros:* fastest visible win; industry-funded; feeds every other scenario; solves "where do resistant students start" (Level 1 is literacy, not ideology). *Cons:* coordination overhead; credit-recognition negotiations.

## 6. The 3-zone AI policy (needed in *every* scenario) — DR23, DR3, DR8

| Zone | Rule | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **AI-required** | deliverables must use AI + disclose method + evaluate output | advanced production courses, capstones (Track B, Scenario 2/3 cores) |
| 🟡 **AI-disclosed** | AI allowed with a disclosure form + making-of evidence | most studio courses |
| 🔴 **AI-free** | live in-class work, foundations courses, admissions portfolios — *and student-authored "craft missions"* | Y1–2 core; Atelier craft missions |

Assessment that survives AI: versioned process files, live critiques, oral defenses (viva), making-of dossiers, growth-rate reviews — **grade the trajectory and the judgment, not just the artifact** (DR2, DR4).

## 7. The Apprenticeship Rebuilt — answering "if AI replaces juniors, how do we grow seniors?"

Six mechanisms, deployable across scenarios:

1. **AI-off craft core (Y1–2):** hands must exist before they can be amplified; you cannot art-direct what you cannot evaluate (DR3).
2. **Judgment notebooks (explicit apprenticeship):** for every AI-assisted task, students record *constraint, trade-off, failure mode checked, evidence used, what to watch in production* — the method that makes invisible apprenticeship visible (architect-to-CTO pattern).
3. **Studio-hierarchy simulation (vertical studio):** seniors direct; juniors execute under supervision — inside the school. Seniors learn direction (the thing with "minimal AI investment"), juniors learn craft + management — the ladder the industry collapsed, rebuilt on campus.
4. **AI-direction rotations:** every student cycles through *evaluating/correcting AI output* (taste), *directing other students* (leadership), and *running pipelines* (execution) — growth rate over productivity (Beam).
5. **Industry-embedded terms:** co-op semester or sponsored Level-3 briefs with TACGA studios; juniors apprentice on real client work with compliance requirements (DR19).
6. **Growth-rate assessment:** quarterly skill-matrix + portfolio-milestone reviews; mentoring targets trajectory, not volume.

## 10. Also worth mentioning

- **Naming:** avoid "AI degree" branding (student stigma + SCAD-style backlash); sell "Creative Direction & Production" with AI inside.
- **Co-governance:** a standing **AI Curriculum Council with student seats** votes the 3-zone policy yearly — converts opponents into owners (DR8).
- **AI changelog:** publish a yearly public changelog of what the curriculum changed and why — trust-building and recruiting.
- **Compute budgeting:** treat local GPUs + open-weight models like lab equipment (sovereignty + NDA-safety + cost stability — the FABW lesson).
- **Research pull:** a small **Creative AI Research Group** (faculty + Atelier students) keeps curriculum current and feeds the master's (KAIST GSCT pattern).
- **KPIs to track from day 1:** employment & role titles at 6/18 months; portfolio process-evidence quality; industry brief wins; student AI-attitude survey delta; alliance enrollment; faculty AI-teaching readiness.
- **Mental-health honesty:** teach the labor data truthfully (Thai contraction, WEF flattening) *alongside* the countervailing evidence (entry roles rebuilt, direction work growing) — anxiety shrinks when the map is honest.
- **Admissions:** keep the DigiPen-style portfolio AI ban for entry (protects the talent signal) while the *degree itself* teaches AI — the two-faced policy every serious school has converged on.
